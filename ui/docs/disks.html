Drives and disks

Drives 8 to 11 are real 1541-family drives with their own 6502, so fast-loaders work. A disk in a drive is a working copy: a SAVE writes to it, as on a real disk, and the old contents go to a history first, so a bad save is never final (☰ next to the drive).

Getting media

  • Drop any .d64 .g64 .d71 .d81 .t64 .tap .crt .prg onto the window, or press Import. They go on your shelf (~/Nix64/shelf), a folder you can see.
  • Library searches the Internet Archive when you ask. You choose what to fetch: make sure you own it or may have it. Nix64 itself never ships ROMs or commercial software.

Using it

  • Drag a disk from the shelf onto a drive slot, or press ▸ beside it for the choices.
  • Load and run puts the disk in drive 8 and types LOAD"*",8,1 for you, where you can watch the real loader. Warp is on while it loads and switches itself off when the machine is ready, then it types RUN.
  • Disks can be saved back to the shelf as a copy, or replaced by a blank formatted one.
  • Tapes (.tap) and cartridges (.crt) have their own slots under the drives.

SID music

  • Drop a .sid file on the window (or Import) and it appears on your shelf with its title, author and song count. Open the SID tab under the screen to play it.
  • Play resets the machine, because a tune takes the whole machine, as on a real C64. If a BASIC program is in memory you are asked first. The tune runs on this machine's own SID chip (the model set by sid.model in its file); Stop resets the machine again.
  • It plays tunes that sit in free RAM. A tune under the ROMs is refused, with the reason, rather than played wrongly. There are no level meters yet. Nix64 ships no music: the High Voltage SID Collection (hvsc.c64.org) is where most of it lives.

Verified by other people

  • Chuck Yeager's Advanced Flight Trainer, from Ken Bandoly (@Maverick_229): a PAL C64C with a 1541-II and true drive emulation, loaded with Load and run, Space at the intro, joystick port 2, Intro Flight reaches the cockpit. His recipe: github.com/MaverickKB/omarchy-chuck-yeager-nix64. It is his report, not yet repeated here, and you need your own copy of the disk.

Honest limits

The datasette has no play/stop/counter controls in the shell yet. Real drives on a ZoomFloppy need the OpenCBM library, which is not in this package (see Real hardware).
